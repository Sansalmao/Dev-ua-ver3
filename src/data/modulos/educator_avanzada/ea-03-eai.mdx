## Strategies for Efficient and Accessible Email Management

<span class="hl">Crucial factors to consider when addressing the storage of EAI mail:</span>

<details>
<summary>The following are crucial factors to consider when addressing the storage of EAI mail</summary>

- **Character Encoding:** UTF-8 encoding is widely used and recommended for handling international characters in email storage.
- **Database and File System Support:**  Ensure that the database or file system used for storing emails supports the storage of Unicode characters. 
- **Validation and Sanitization:** Validate email addresses in accordance with the relevant standards outlined in RFC 6530 and other relevant IETF specifications.
- **Indexing and Search:** Ensure that the indexing mechanism supports Unicode characters and can handle search queries involving non-ASCII characters effectively.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Transitioning to EAI: Updates and Changes in Email Protocols</summary>

**Key protocol changes needed for enabling EAI:**
- **SMTPUTF8:** Local part and Domain part can be UTF-8.
**MIME Updates:** email headers, subject lines, and body content can be UTF-8.
- **Punycode Encoding:** encoding and transmitting non-ASCII characters in a compatible format, ensuring proper handling and delivery of EAI messages.
- **Address Parsing and Validation:** updates in email protocols include enhancements to address parsing and validation mechanisms.
- **Compatibility Guidelines:** e-mail protocol updates also include the development of compatibility guidelines and best practices for implementing EAI.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the SMTP Signaling Flags and SMTPUTF8 Extension?</summary>

**SMTP Signaling Flags and the SMTPUTF8 extension:**
- **SMTP Signaling Flags:**
  - **8BITMIME:** this flag indicates that the SMTP client can handle 8-bit data, which is necessary for the proper representation of internationalized characters.
  - **ENHANCEDSTATUSCODES:** are used to provide more detailed information about the processing of internationalized email addresses.
  - **UTF8CLIENT:** indicates that the SMTP client can handle UTF-8 encoded data,
- **SMTPUTF8 Extension:** SMTPUTF8 keyword within the EHLO command.

</details>

<details>
<summary>EAI Enabled POP/IMAP Infrastructure: Requirements for Clients and Servers</summary>

**Key considerations related to EAI for email service providers and organizations:**
- **Encoding and Character Set Support:** Email systems should be capable of accurately storing, transmitting, and displaying non-ASCII characters.
- **Address Parsing and Validation:** Implementing robust algorithms and mechanisms for parsing and validating EAI is vital.
- **MIME Compatibility and Content Handling:** Email systems should handle encoding, decoding, and rendering of internationalized content, including subject lines, headers, attachments, and text in various languages and scripts.
- **SMTPUTF8 Support.**
- **Compliance with EAI Standards and Guidelines.**
- **User Interface Adaptation.**

</details>

<details>
<summary>Considerations for EAI in Mail Message Formats</summary>

**The following aspects of mail message formats are particularly affected by EAI:**
- **Header Fields:** Support for non-ASCII characters in the sender and recipient fields.
- **Subject Line:** EAI enables the use of non-ASCII characters in the subject line of email messages.
- **Message Body:** EAI supports the inclusion of non-ASCII characters in the message body.
- **Attachments:**
  - EAI does not impose any specific changes to attachments themselves. 
  - However, the filenames of attachments can contain non-ASCII characters encoded in UTF-8.
- **MIME:** MIME headers and content types can incorporate internationalized email addresses, and the encoding mechanisms can handle non-ASCII characters.

</details>

## How non-ASCII Characters can be Encoded in Email Addresses

<div class="info-card">

<span class="hl">Consider the email address: café@example.com</span>
- <span class="hl">Encoding Non-ASCII Character: "é" of the email address:</span>
  - **Represent the character in Unicode:** The Unicode code point for "é" is U+00E9.
  - **Convert the Unicode code point to binary:** In this case, the binary representation of U+00E9 is 11000011 10010101.
  - **Determine the number of bytes needed in UTF-8:** Based on the binary representation, we can see that it falls into the range of U+0080 to U+07FF, which requires 2 bytes in UTF-8 encoding.
  - **Apply the UTF-8 encoding rules:**
    - For a 2-byte sequence, the leading bits of the first byte are "110" and the leading bits of the second byte are "10".
    - **Distribute the bits of the Unicode code point into the available positions in the bytes:**
      - The first byte: 11000011
      - The second byte: 10010101
    - **Convert the binary representation back to hexadecimal:**
      - The first byte: 11000011 → C3 (in hexadecimal).
      - The second byte: 10010101 → A9 (in hexadecimal).
    - **Replace the non-ASCII character "é" with its UTF-8 encoded representation:**
      - café@example.com becomes caf%C3%A9@example.com.

</div>

## EAI Adoption Challenges: a Temporary Stop-Gap Solution

- <span class="hl">Address Translation/Transliteration: </span> The receiving email client detects an internationalized email address that is not fully compatible with its system. It identifies the non-ASCII characters in the address.
- <span class="hl">Translation/Transliteration Mapping:</span> The email client utilizes a mapping or lookup mechanism to translate or transliterate the non-ASCII characters into ASCII characters or a supported character set.
- <span class="hl">Display and Processing:</span> The translated or transliterated address is displayed in the email client's interface, allowing the user to view and interact with it.
- <span class="hl">Reverse Translation for Replies:</span> When the user replies or forwards an email with a translated address, the email client can perform a reverse translation or transliteration process to restore the original internationalized email address.
- <span class="hl">User Customization:</span> The translation or transliteration feature can provide options for user customization.
- <span class="hl">Compatibility Notifications:</span> The email client can notify the user when a translated or transliterated address is encountered, indicating that the displayed address is not the original representation.
